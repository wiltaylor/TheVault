# Android distribution for an app reading SMS, calls and contacts

## Question

The Android client is built with Tauri 2. It wants READ_SMS, RECEIVE_SMS, RECEIVE_MMS, READ_CALL_LOG (and possibly READ_PHONE_STATE or call screening), READ/WRITE_CONTACTS, POST_NOTIFICATIONS and background work. It will not be the default SMS or dialer app. How can it be distributed: through Google Play, by sideloading on Android 13 to 16, through F-Droid, or through Obtainium and GitHub Releases? What does each route require?

## Answer

1. **Google Play is not a realistic route.** Play only allows SMS and Call Log permissions in an app that is not the default handler under a fixed list of temporary exceptions. Each one needs a Permissions Declaration Form and a review. "Log my messages into my personal notes" does not fit any exception. The closest ones are "Backup and restore", "Cross-device synchronization or transfer of SMS or calls" and "Enterprise archive, business & enterprise CRM". Each covers a narrower purpose than ours, so a rejection is likely. The app would also have to be public, or use closed testing, and pay the developer fee. A single-user app gains nothing from that.
2. **Sideloading works, but the user must unlock the SMS permission by hand.** Android 13 added "restricted settings" for apps installed from a downloaded or local file, such as a browser or file manager install. Android 15's CDD extends the restricted list to the **SMS runtime permission** and the default SMS and dialer roles. Accessibility, notification listener, device admin, overlay and usage access were already on it. The user unlocks them in App info, ⋮ menu, "Allow restricted settings". The sources do not name call log or contacts as restricted. Apps installed by a store through the session installer API are exempt from restricted settings.
3. **Developer verification (2026 to 2027) does not block a personal install.** Installing over ADB is exempt. The free "Limited Distribution" account needs no ID and covers up to 20 devices. Enforcement started on 2026-09-30, only in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand and only for participating stores. Direct sideloads are not covered until the global rollout in 2027. Power users get an "advanced flow" to allow unverified apps: Developer Options, a restart, a 24-hour wait, then access for 7 days or indefinitely.
4. **F-Droid is possible but costly.** The app must be FLOSS, F-Droid builds it from source, and it may not use Google Play Services, Firebase or FCM. Rust toolchains are allowed. A Tauri app needs Node and Rust in one F-Droid build recipe. Reproducible builds are optional, but F-Droid can only ship the developer's own signature if its rebuild matches the developer's APK byte for byte.
5. **Recommendation:** do not target Play. Sign release APKs with our own long-lived keystore and attach them to GitHub (or Forgejo) Releases. Install with `adb install`, or have Obtainium track the releases. Treat F-Droid as optional and later. Register a free Limited Distribution account before the 2027 global enforcement, or rely on ADB or the advanced flow. Document the "Allow restricted settings" step in the onboarding screen for the SMS permission.

## Why

- Play SMS/Call Log policy and its exceptions: Account verification via phone call, anti-smishing, backup and restore, caller ID and spam, connected-device companion apps, cross-device sync or transfer of SMS or calls, device automation, enterprise archive and business/enterprise CRM, in-vehicle use, physical safety alerts, proxy calls, cell broadcast, SMS financial transactions, call-based bank authentication, SMS-based money management, writing call history for the default dialer, and SYSTEM_UI_INTELLIGENCE. An exception requires that the permission enables core functionality and that "there's currently no alternative method". The Permissions Declaration Form must stay accurate. Breaking the policy risks suspension. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10208820
- Restricted settings and how the user allows them: https://support.google.com/android/answer/12623953
- What Android 15 adds (the SMS runtime permission, the SMS and dialer roles, and Enhanced Confirmation Mode allowlists): https://www.androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098/ , https://www.androidpolice.com/android-15-enhanced-confirmation-mode-sideloading/ , https://9to5google.com/2024/09/12/android-15-sideloaded-apps-restrictions/ . The ECM API is in AOSP: https://android.googlesource.com/platform/prebuilts/fullsdk/sources/+/refs/heads/androidx-xr-arcore-release/android-35/android/app/ecm/EnhancedConfirmationManager.java . A developer's report that the SMS toggle is greyed out for sideloaded apps on Android 15 and 16: https://textbee.dev/blog/android-15-send-sms-permission-guide
- Developer verification overview, timeline and Limited Distribution accounts: https://developer.android.com/developer-verification , https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/android-developer-verification-rolling-out-to-all-developers.html . The FAQ: "you are free to install apps without verification with ADB". The $25 fee applies only to Full Distribution. "If ... users sideload your app directly, these new verification requirements won't apply to your app yet". The advanced flow has a 24-hour wait. https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides/faq
- F-Droid inclusion policy (FLOSS only, built from source, no Play Services or Firebase, Rust/rustup prebuilt toolchains acceptable): https://f-droid.org/docs/Inclusion_Policy/
- F-Droid reproducible builds: optional. Developer-signed publishing (`Binaries` plus `AllowedAPKSigningKeys`) only publishes when F-Droid's rebuild matches. https://f-droid.org/docs/Reproducible_Builds/
- Practical notes on getting a Tauri app into F-Droid: commit `src-tauri/gen/android`, ship fastlane metadata, and choose the signing key before submitting, because F-Droid-signed and GitHub-signed APKs cannot update each other. https://github.com/TheMostafaOsamaDev/Riwaq-Reader/pull/141
- Obtainium installs and updates apps from GitHub, GitLab or F-Droid release pages and checks APK signing certificates. https://obtainium.imranr.dev/ , https://github.com/ImranR98/Obtainium/releases

## Consequences

- **No Play-specific work.** Skip Play policy compliance, the declaration form, Play Integrity and FCM. Use no Google Play Services, which keeps F-Droid open and suits a self-hosted app. Background sync must not rely on FCM push. Use WorkManager, a foreground service, or the RECEIVE_SMS broadcast to trigger a sync to the server.
- **Onboarding must handle restricted settings.** Request SMS, call log and contacts at runtime. If the SMS grant is blocked, show the user how to use App info, ⋮, "Allow restricted settings", then grant again. Expect that a phone maker or a later Android release may also restrict call log.
- **Avoid accessibility, notification listener and call screening unless they are essential.** Call screening (CallScreeningService) needs the call-screening role. Not verified: whether Android restricts that role for sideloaded apps the way it restricts the SMS and dialer roles. READ_CALL_LOG plus READ_PHONE_STATE is enough to log calls after they happen.
- **Treat the signing key as permanent.** Generate it once with `keytool -genkeypair` (RSA 4096, validity of 25 years or more), keep the keystore and passwords outside the repository, and back them up offline. Sign with `apksigner sign`, check with `apksigner verify --print-certs`, and configure Tauri's Gradle signingConfig through a `keystore.properties` file that is not committed. A lost key means an uninstall and reinstall, which deletes local app data. Record the certificate SHA-256 in the README so Obtainium or F-Droid `AllowedAPKSigningKeys` can pin it.
- **Prepare for verification in 2027.** Register the package name and signing key under a free Limited Distribution account (up to 20 devices, enough for one user). ADB installs stay exempt either way.
- **F-Droid later, if wanted.** It needs a FLOSS licence and a committed Gradle project. For the developer signature to ship, the Rust and Vite builds must be reproducible: pinned toolchains, `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH`, and deterministic output.
